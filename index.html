What is MRI?

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is a non-invasive imaging technology that produces highly detailed images of the internal structures of the body, including organs, tissues, and the brain.

MRI uses powerful magnetic fields and radio waves instead of harmful X-rays, making it safe for repeated examinations. It is widely used for diagnosing diseases such as tumors, brain disorders, spinal injuries, and cardiovascular problems.

Different MRI sequences, such as T1, T2, and FLAIR, provide distinct information about tissue composition and pathology.

AI in MRI Analysis

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming MRI analysis by automating image interpretation, detecting anomalies, and assisting radiologists with faster and more accurate diagnostics.

Deep learning models can learn patterns from thousands of MRI images to detect tumors, brain lesions, and other abnormalities. This reduces human error and enables early diagnosis, improving patient outcomes.

MRI Segmentation

Segmentation divides MRI images into meaningful regions, helping measure tissue volumes, track disease progression, and plan treatments. Accurate segmentation is critical for brain, tumor, and organ analysis.

Brain Segmentation

Separates gray matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid for precise brain analysis.

Tumor Segmentation

Detects tumor boundaries accurately to aid in treatment planning and monitoring.

Organ Segmentation

Segments organs such as liver, kidneys, and heart for better diagnosis and research.
